D-NeRF monocular benchmark · Tesla T4

Three dynamic Gaussian Splatting methods, measured twice

Every scene was trained under two protocols: an equal iteration budget (30 000 steps for all methods) and an equal loss budget (stop as soon as the evaluation L1 reaches a common per-scene target). The first asks which method reconstructs best; the second asks what the same reconstruction quality costs.

Read the numbers with these three caveats

  1. Lego cannot be evaluated for 4DGS native-4D. The run produces no evaluation entry — a known limitation already reported in the original paper, not a failure of this benchmark. It is excluded from every 4DGS native-4D figure.
  2. T-Rex and Jumping Jacks fixed-iteration runs of 4DGS native-4D are partial (stopped at 6 000 and 15 000 steps). They had reached their practical ceiling on a T4: the primitive count grows to 8.4·10⁵ and 9.8·10⁵ and a thousand iterations costs 1 062 s and 772 s respectively. Marked with ✳.
  3. Because 4DGS native-4D peaks early and then degrades, the best value of each metric over a run is compared, not the final one — which is also what the partial runs allow.
  4. An iteration is not the same unit of work for every method. 4DGS native-4D uses the per-scene batch of its own repository (1 to 24); the other two always use 1. Conclusions

What the two protocols say once every run is on the table. Averages are over the five scenes completed by all three methods.

If maximum quality matters and time is not the constraint

Deformable-3DGS

It wins on every scene and every metric, with a margin of about 3 dB (40.76 vs 37.81 dB mean PSNR at 30 000 steps). At 30 000 iterations it has not yet saturated its own original default of 40 000, so this comparison slightly penalises it. The price is twice the training time (36.7 vs 18.8 min) and the highest VRAM of the three (3.3 GB).

If the quality/cost ratio matters

4DGaussians

Second in absolute quality, but half of Deformable's training time, the lowest VRAM (1.6 GB) and the lowest time to reach a fixed quality target (3.5 vs 6.7 min). It is the natural choice with limited resources, or whenever fast iteration is what matters.

Its model on disk, however, carries a fixed component due to the HexPlane grid, independent of the number of primitives: on simple scenes it can therefore occupy more than Deformable while holding fewer primitives (17.9 vs 12.2 MB at the target).

Fewest steps, highest cost

4DGS with native 4D primitives

It converges in very few optimisation steps (5 258 against roughly 8 300 to the same L1 target), but on this dataset it is the most expensive in time, in model size and in number of primitives, and it is the only method that degrades if left training past its own optimum (−0.62 dB from the peak). On the scenes with large batches it is not even completable on a T4.

The limit does not appear to be implementational but structural: native 4D primitives are penalised by the monocular case, where every timestamp offers a single view, and the authors themselves state that they introduce no architectural priors specific to this case.

Across all methods

The practical constraint is time, not memory

Peak VRAM never exceeded 5.1 GB of the 16 available on the T4. What decides the feasibility of a run is wall-clock time.
